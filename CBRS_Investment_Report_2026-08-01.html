Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

▾
Cerebras is the purest public bet on the single most important shift in AI economics — from training to inference — and it owns a genuinely differentiated weapon: the wafer-scale engine, a dinner-plate-sized chip with ~900,000 cores that delivers order-of-magnitude faster token generation than clustered GPUs. The technology is real, the growth is explosive (revenue +76% to $510M in 2025, first-ever profit of $238M), and the backlog is stunning ($20B+ OpenAI deal, an AWS partnership). But at ~72× trailing sales with ~86% of revenue historically tied to two Gulf-linked customers, this is a high-variance, small-position instrument, not a core holding. We rate it a Speculative Buy — own the option on inference winning, size it for the concentration risk.

The asymmetry here is the opposite of Tesla's. Where Tesla is a mega-cap priced for autonomy, Cerebras is a small-float, freshly-IPO'd disruptor priced for hyperscale adoption that has barely begun. If Cerebras converts even a fraction of its OpenAI/AWS backlog and diversifies away from G42/MBZUAI, the revenue base could 3–5× and the multiple would look prescient. If the concentrated customers slip or a capacity-constrained ramp stalls, the stock has 60%+ downside. The P/S of ~72× ("Euphoric" on our scale) tells you the market is already underwriting the good outcome.

Microeconomic & Valuation Analysis

▾

Cerebras sits in the fastest-compounding slice of the AI stack. The AI inference market is projected to grow from ~$106B (2025) to ~$255B by 2030 — a ~19% CAGR — and inference (running models) is structurally larger and more recurring than training (building them). Cerebras' pitch: for latency-sensitive inference, one wafer-scale system replaces racks of networked GPUs, collapsing cost and complexity. Capturing even low-single-digit share of a $255B market implies a multi-billion-dollar revenue base.

Valuation posture

There is no gentle way to say it: at ~72× trailing sales (and ~54× forward), Cerebras is priced in the "Euphoric" zone of our P/S scale (>30). That is nearly double Nvidia's ~30× forward sales — and Nvidia is a proven, ~50%-net-margin hyperscale supplier. The premium is a bet on (a) the inference-first thesis, (b) hyperscale-grade backlog converting to revenue, and (c) small-float scarcity after the largest semiconductor IPO ever ($6.4B raised). This multiple leaves zero margin for execution error.

Qualitative Analysis — Moat, Management & Culture

▾

The moat, philosophically. Cerebras' edge is architectural, not incremental. By building a single wafer-scale chip (the WSE-3) instead of stitching thousands of GPUs together with networking, it sidesteps the memory-bandwidth and interconnect bottlenecks that define GPU clusters. For real-time inference this yields dramatically faster token throughput. The durable question: is wafer-scale a defensible platform (like x86 or CUDA) or a point-in-time performance lead that GPUs, ASICs (Broadcom/Google TPU) and rivals (Groq's LPU, SambaNova's RDU) eventually erode? The honest answer is "unproven" — the moat is a technical head-start plus early software ecosystem, not yet a lock-in.

Management & founder dynamics

Emphatically founder-led. Co-founder/CEO Andrew Feldman (previously sold SeaMicro to AMD) leads a deep bench of the original 2015 founding team (Sean Lie, Gary Lauterbach, et al.) — rare technical continuity a decade in. This is elite silicon talent density. Capital allocation is now flush post-IPO ($6.4B raised), which funds the manufacturing scale-up needed to relieve the capacity constraint that is currently capping revenue (guidance is capacity-limited, not demand-limited — a good problem).

Structural traits
Extreme talent density in chip architecture; a genuine first-principles engineering culture (they built something the entire industry said was impossible — a working wafer-scale chip). High failure tolerance and fast iteration. The organizational risk is the mirror image: a still-young company scaling manufacturing, sales, and a public-company apparatus simultaneously, with heavy dependence on a small circle of founders and anchor customers.

Culture & ethics — the elephant. The deep G42 (UAE) relationship is both the growth engine and the governance question. It creates geopolitical exposure (export controls, national-security scrutiny of Gulf AI capital) and concentration that a serious analyst cannot wave away. Management's stated strategy — diversify via OpenAI, AWS and enterprise — is the correct answer; execution is the open question.

Quantitative Analysis — Financial Deep Dive

▾

The financial trajectory is one of the steepest in public semis. Revenue went $24.6M (2022) → $78.7M (2023) → $290.3M (2024) → $510.0M (2025), a ~174% three-year CAGR. More importantly, 2025 flipped to a $237.8M net profit from a $481.6M loss in 2024 — the inference ramp is not just growing, it's operating-leveraged. Q1 2026 kept the pace: GAAP revenue $193.4M, core revenue $191.3M (+92% YoY).

Revenue streams, in plain English

Cerebras sells two things: systems (the CS-3 / wafer-scale hardware, sold or deployed in racks) and, increasingly, cloud inference (renting access to that speed via its own cloud and partners like AWS). The 2024→2025 story was G42-anchored systems deployments; the 2026+ story management is selling is a shift toward recurring, diversified inference-as-a-service revenue (OpenAI 750MW / $20B+ multi-year; AWS distribution). If that mix shift is real, gross margins (guided 36–38% near-term) should expand and revenue quality improves markedly.

Read the margin footnote carefully
The 2025 net income of $238M is flattered by one-off items and the mix of large systems deals; Q2'26 operating margin is guided to −30% to −32% as the company invests to scale. In other words: GAAP profitability is real but lumpy, and the near-term operating model is still investment-mode. Flywheel & operating leverage

The intended flywheel: faster inference → more developers/enterprises adopt → more cloud usage → funds bigger fabs → lower cost per token → faster inference. It is early but visibly turning (revenue +76%, first profit, capacity-constrained demand). The risk to the flywheel is capital intensity — wafer-scale manufacturing is expensive and lumpy, which is exactly what the $6.4B IPO war-chest is meant to smooth.

Risks & Anti-Thesis (Steelman the Bear)

▾

If I were short CBRS, this is the case — and it is a strong one.

1. Customer concentration is the whole company

G42 was 85% of 2024 revenue; G42 + MBZUAI ~86% of 2025. A single anchor customer delay, dispute, or geopolitical restriction could wipe out a huge slice of revenue overnight. This is the highest-probability, highest-impact risk and it is only partly offset by the (still-unproven) OpenAI/AWS pipeline.

Prob: HighImpact: SeverePriced in: Partially

2. The valuation demands flawless execution

~72× trailing / ~54× forward sales versus Nvidia at ~30× forward. Any growth stumble triggers brutal multiple compression. At these levels you can be right on the business and still lose money for years as the multiple normalizes.

Prob: HighImpact: HighPriced in: No

3. Nvidia + ASICs close the inference gap

Nvidia is pouring resources into inference; Broadcom/Google (TPU), Groq and SambaNova all target the same workloads. Wafer-scale's speed lead is a head-start, not a CUDA-style moat. If GPUs get "fast enough," the differentiation — and premium — erodes.

Prob: MediumImpact: HighPriced in: Poorly

4. Geopolitical / export-control exposure

Deep Gulf (UAE/G42) ties invite national-security scrutiny and potential export restrictions on advanced AI hardware. A policy shift could sever a core revenue channel with little warning — a fat-tail risk outside management's control.

Prob: Low-MedImpact: SeverePriced in: No

5. Lock-up expiry & capital intensity

Post-IPO lock-up expirations will add share supply into a thin float, a mechanical headwind later in 2026. Meanwhile wafer-scale manufacturing is capital-heavy and lumpy; a capacity mis-step could turn a "good problem" (constrained supply) into missed revenue and margin volatility.

Why we're still a (small) buyer
The inference-first thesis is, in our view, correct, and Cerebras owns the most differentiated hardware expression of it with real revenue, real profit, and hyperscale validation (OpenAI/AWS). That combination is rare. The concentration and valuation risks are exactly why this is a small, speculative position sized to survive a 60% drawdown — not a core holding.

Glossary & Methodology

▾

P/S — Price-to-Sales

P/S = Market Cap ÷ Revenue (TTM)

Revenue multiple, the key gauge for pre-scale, high-growth companies. Scale used: >30 Euphoric · >18 Excited · >8 Optimistic · 4–8 Aware · <4 Cautious · <2 Pessimistic. CBRS ≈ 72× → "Euphoric."

↑ back to valuation

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate

CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1/years) − 1

The smoothed annual growth rate over a period. Why it matters: Cerebras' ~174% three-year revenue CAGR is what the market is extrapolating; the AI-inference market's ~19% CAGR is the tailwind it rides.

↑ back to valuation

Inference vs. Training

Training = building/teaching an AI model (compute-heavy, episodic). Inference = running the trained model to answer queries (recurring, latency-sensitive, larger long-run market). Cerebras is optimized for fast inference — the structurally bigger, more recurring pool. ↑ back

Wafer-Scale Engine (WSE)

Cerebras' signature chip: an entire silicon wafer (~900,000 cores) used as one processor, versus stitching thousands of GPUs together. Eliminates most chip-to-chip networking, boosting inference speed. The source of the moat — and the capital intensity. ↑ back
