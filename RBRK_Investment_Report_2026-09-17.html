Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

▾
Rubrik has quietly become the most reliable executor in security software: eight consecutive quarters beating every guided metric, subscription ARR compounding 33% at a $1.66B scale, net new ARR accelerating (+35% YoY), and free cash flow swinging from −$21M to a guided $323–333M in two fiscal years. The company has also repositioned from "backup vendor" to "agentic cyber resilience" — data, identity and AI-agent security on one platform — at precisely the moment frontier AI models made machine-speed attacks a board-level fear. Our contrarian point is not that the business is weak; it is that the stock (+140% off its 52-week low, 25 of 28 analysts at Strong Buy, 14× trailing sales) already discounts most of the good news, while stock-based compensation still exceeds free cash flow. We rate RBRK a Quality Compounder — Accumulate on Weakness: build the position below ~$90, hold the core, and do not chase.

The asymmetry is about durability, not discovery. The Street already sees the ARR growth; the debate that will determine 3-to-5-year returns is (a) whether Rubrik can keep net new ARR growing as the legacy-to-cloud migration tailwind — which management says "wraps up this year" — rolls off, and (b) whether Identity Resilience and Rubrik Agent Cloud (RAC) become second and third growth engines large enough to sustain 25%+ growth into FY29. If yes, RBRK re-rates toward CrowdStrike-style scarcity multiples and compounds at 20%+. If no, the company becomes a very good 18–20% grower whose multiple compresses toward Zscaler's ~9× sales, and the stock goes sideways for years. Our probability-weighted five-year return is roughly 11% annualized — a solid business at a full price.

Microeconomic & Valuation Analysis

▾

Rubrik sells cyber resilience — the ability to keep operating and recover cleanly when (not if) an attack lands. Its core market, enterprise backup and data protection, is mature but being violently re-priced: every ransomware event converts a "storage line item" into a "board-level insurance policy." Third-party estimates size the data protection & recovery software segment at ~$11B in 2026 growing ~17.5% annually, while the broader data resiliency market (which includes cyber recovery, identity resilience and business-continuity services) is estimated at ~$45B in 2026 heading toward ~$167B by 2034 (~18% CAGR). Rubrik's own IPO-era framing put its serviceable TAM at roughly $53B by 2027; the identity (Strata.io, Okta/AD/Entra recovery) and AI-agent security (RAC) expansions add adjacencies on top of that. At $1.66B of ARR, Rubrik has low-single-digit share of the wide definition and mid-teens share of the narrow one — there is room, but the narrow core is a share-take fight against Veeam, Cohesity (now merged with Veritas), Commvault and Dell.

Qualitative Analysis — Moat, Management & Culture

▾

The moat, philosophically. Backup is the one security product that is guaranteed to be used in anger. When ransomware detonates, the recovery platform becomes the company's last line of truth — and switching that platform mid-crisis is unthinkable. Rubrik's structural edge is that it sits on top of the crown-jewel data itself (immutable, air-gapped copies across cloud, SaaS and on-prem), which makes it the natural place to layer higher-value services: threat hunting on the backup copy, sensitive-data discovery, identity recovery for Active Directory/Entra/Okta, and now observability and "rewind" for AI agents. Each layer increases data gravity and the cost of leaving. The result shows up in the numbers — net retention above 119% and $1M+ customers growing 57% and now 88% of ARR. This is a switching-cost and data-gravity moat, reinforced by a seven-year run as a Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader (2026: furthest in Vision). What it is not is a network-effect moat: Cohesity/Veritas, Veeam, Commvault and Dell all sell credible immutable backup, and hyperscalers ship "good enough" native tooling.

Founder-led, with concentrated control. CEO/Chairman Bipul Sinha co-founded Rubrik in 2014 after a career as a venture investor (Lightspeed) and engineer; co-founder Arvind Nithrakashyap remains CTO. Class B super-voting stock keeps control with the founders and early backers — good for long-horizon decisions, weaker for shareholder accountability. CFO Kiran Choudary (since 2020) has established a textbook "beat-and-raise" cadence, and the board was recently broadened with Rakefet Russak-Aminoach (former Bank Leumi CEO) for financial-services and governance depth. The revenue organization is in transition: Jesse Green (ex-MongoDB, AppDynamics) was promoted to CRO in February 2026, replacing Brian McCarthy, and the 10-K explicitly flags "the leadership transition in our global revenue organization" as a risk. So far the numbers say the handoff is working — Q2 net new ARR accelerated under Green.

Capital allocation. Rubrik is in "reinvest everything" mode: no buybacks, no dividends, three tuck-in acquisitions in 14 months (Predibase for AI model tuning/inference in mid-2025; Strata.io for identity orchestration in 2026; a smaller 2025 deal), a £375M/$500M UK investment with London as EMEA HQ, and a $1.15B convertible raised in June 2025 at essentially zero coupon. The convert was smart — it refinanced ~$330M of expensive term debt, funded acquisitions and left ~$600M of net cash — but the equity story is still funded by dilution: shares outstanding grew 6.6% YoY and SBC ran $342M over the trailing four quarters (22% of revenue). Management guides to 228M weighted shares for FY27; watch whether that rises.

Culture & organizational traits. Rubrik scores well on innovation pace — RAC went from launch (Oct 2025) to GA (Q4 FY26) to 15+ paying customers and four product pillars (Observability, Identity, Runtime Security, Rewind) in under a year, and the company was an early design partner on Anthropic's Mythos preview ("Project Glasswing") and then shipped Code Guardian on top of it within months. Customer obsession shows in the 5,000-hospital AHA partnership and the "Minimum Viable Hospital / Minimum Viable Business" recovery framing, which is genuinely how CISOs think. On talent density and idea meritocracy we have less visibility; revenue per employee (~$406K) trails CrowdStrike (~$504K) and Palo Alto (~$524K), suggesting the sales motion is still people-heavy. Failure tolerance is evident in the willingness to buy and re-platform (Predibase → Agent Rewind → RAC) rather than build slowly. Ethics: no material controversies; the main governance flag is dual-class control.

Structural read
Rubrik is a founder-controlled, product-velocity organization that has learned to run a disciplined public-company cadence. The culture is an asset for the AI pivot; the dual-class structure and SBC-funded growth are the costs of that culture.

Quantitative Analysis — Financial Deep Dive

▾

Revenue streams, explained simply

Rubrik has two lines: Subscription (95% of revenue — Rubrik Security Cloud SaaS, RSC-Private term licenses, and standalone SaaS add-ons like Anomaly Detection and Sensitive Data Monitoring) and Other (5% — professional services, hardware appliances, legacy maintenance). Subscription revenue grew 37% YoY in Q2 FY27 to $407.2M; total revenue grew 38% to $427.3M (43% normalized for "material rights," a non-cash accounting item from legacy customers' migration options that is shrinking from $14.2M a year ago to $4.7M). Geographically, international revenue grew 52% to $129M while the Americas grew 33% to $299M — the London HQ investment is paying off. Sequentially, revenue grew 10.4% QoQ, the strongest sequential quarter in the post-IPO history.

Free cash flow — the real story

Rubrik's FCF turned positive in Q3 FY25 and has now been positive for eight straight quarters: $15.6M → $75.2M → $33.3M → $57.5M → $76.9M → $70.1M → $73.6M → $65.7M. Trailing-four-quarter FCF is $286M (18.6% FCF margin), versus $21.6M for all of FY25 and $237.8M for FY26. Management raised FY27 FCF guidance twice this year, from $265–275M to $323–333M (~19.5% margin). Note the seasonality and the Q2 dip: FCF margin fell from 19% to 15% YoY in Q2 because of $53M of taxes paid on net share settlement of RSUs — a cost of SBC that is now flowing through the financing line rather than the operating line. FCF per share has risen from ~$0.11 (FY25) to ~$1.38 (TTM), a phenomenal ramp — but with 6.6% annual share growth, per-share compounding will lag headline FCF growth by that amount every year.

Owner-earnings check
TTM FCF of $286M minus TTM SBC of $342M = −$56M. On a fully-burdened basis Rubrik is still roughly breakeven. The trend is right — SBC as a share of revenue has fallen from 69% (FY25, IPO-driven) to 25% (FY26) to 22% (TTM) — but the "16% FCF margin" headline overstates true economic profitability until SBC dips below ~12–15% of revenue.

Margins, operating leverage and the flywheel

Non-GAAP gross margin is 81% (down ~60 bps YoY on lower material-rights revenue and mix; Q1 was a record 82.9%). GAAP operating margin improved from −30% to −17% YoY; non-GAAP operating margin swung from −1.4% to +7.8%. The cleanest lens on leverage is Rubrik's own subscription ARR contribution margin, which has expanded every single quarter for two years: −10.6% → −8.2% → −3.3% → 2.1% → 8.0% → 9.4% → 10.3% → 11.6% → 13.2% → 14.0%, guided to ~15.5% for FY27. Revenue is growing 38% while non-GAAP operating expenses grew 21% (R&D +34%, S&M +23%, G&A −8%) — that 17-point gap is operating leverage in its purest form. The flywheel: land with backup/cyber-recovery → expand into identity, SaaS apps (M365, Google Workspace, Salesforce) and cloud databases → net retention >119% drives ARR without proportional sales cost → cash funds product/AI expansion → bigger platform wins bigger "Rubrik Flex" enterprise contracts (now the default motion for strategic accounts).

Growth rates, balance sheet and liquidity

Revenue: FY22 $506M → FY23 $600M → FY24 $628M (the SaaS transition trough) → FY25 $887M → FY26 $1,316M → FY27 guide ~$1,689M. Net margin remains negative on a GAAP basis (−16.5% TTM) entirely because of SBC; strip it out and the business earned ~$0.50/share over the last four quarters.

The balance sheet is strong but unusual: $1.75B of cash and short-term investments, $1.13B of 0% convertible notes due 2030, ~$600M net cash, and negative stockholders' equity (−$499M) because deferred revenue ($1.93B, current and non-current) sits as a liability against cash already collected. That deferred revenue is the good kind — prepaid multi-year subscriptions. Current ratio is 1.64, working capital ~$888M, and interest income ($16M/quarter) now exceeds interest expense by 15×. Accounts receivable rose only 5% YoY on 38% revenue growth — collections are excellent. Shares outstanding: 207.3M (+6.6% YoY, +1.2% QoQ); non-GAAP diluted count 226.8M including the convert's if-converted shares.

Risks & Anti-Thesis — Steelmanning the Short

▾

If we were short RBRK at $105, these are the five arguments we would lead with. Each is engaged on its merits.

1. The growth you are paying for was partly a one-time migration

Cloud ARR grew 39% but adjusted net new Cloud ARR — stripping out legacy customers migrating to hosted products — grew only 20%. The CFO said the migration "wraps up this year." Q4 FY26's record $115M net new ARR and the 34% ARR growth rate both leaned on that tailwind. A short would argue FY28 net new ARR growth reverts to the high-teens, the ARR growth rate slides toward 22–25%, and a 14× sales multiple built on "33% and accelerating" breaks. The bull rebuttal: total net new subscription ARR (+35%) is what matters, and non-cloud (RSC-Private, sovereign) demand is real. We split the difference — this is the single most important risk.

Probability: 45%Impact: High (−30–40%)Priced in: Partially

2. Stock-based compensation makes the "profitable" narrative fictional

TTM SBC of $342M exceeds TTM FCF of $286M. GAAP net loss was $62M last quarter and $254M TTM. Shares grew 6.6% in a year and management guides to 228M weighted shares — the diluted count is already 226.8M. A short frames it simply: shareholders are paying employees $340M/yr in equity and being told the company generates $300M of "free" cash. If the stock falls, retention pressure forces more SBC, not less — a reflexive loop. Rebuttal: SBC/revenue has fallen from 69% to 22% in two years and is on a path to the mid-teens; at 25%+ growth the dilution is absorbed. We think this caps the multiple rather than breaks the thesis.

Probability: 70% (persists)Impact: MediumPriced in: Mostly

3. Rubrik Agent Cloud is a story, not a business — and the platforms will own it

RAC has "over 15 paying customers" and management assumes "minimum" RAC contribution to FY27 ARR. Yet the AI-agent security narrative is doing meaningful work in the multiple. Agent observability, guardrails and identity-for-agents are being built natively by Microsoft (Copilot Studio/Entra Agent ID), Google (Gemini Enterprise), AWS (Bedrock AgentCore), CrowdStrike, Palo Alto and Okta — the same vendors Rubrik must integrate with. A short says Rubrik's edge ("rewind" from backup data) is one feature, not a platform, and that the Predibase acquisition was a pivot searching for a product. Rebuttal: rewind and clean-recovery are exactly what platforms lack, and early GSI alliances (Cognizant, Deloitte, HCL, NTT, Wipro) suggest real pull. Verdict: high uncertainty, modest near-term financial impact.

Probability: 50%Impact: Medium (narrative)Priced in: No

4. Backup is commoditizing beneath the platform layer

Cohesity's merger with Veritas created a scaled rival with a larger installed base; Veeam remains the volume leader and is moving upmarket; Commvault grows ~17% at one-third of Rubrik's multiple; Dell, HPE and the hyperscalers bundle "good enough" immutable backup. Rubrik's Q2 gross margin slipped 60 bps YoY and management cited "mix." A short argues price competition in the core will pressure gross margin and force higher S&M just as growth decelerates — the classic SaaS margin squeeze. Rebuttal: net retention >119% and $1M+ customers growing 57% say enterprises are consolidating onto Rubrik, not away. We view this as a slow-burn risk that matters more in years 3–5 than year 1.

Probability: 35%Impact: Medium-HighPriced in: No

5. Crowded positioning, unanimous Street, and a stock that already ran 140%

25 of 28 analysts say Strong Buy; the stock is 18% above its 50-day and 50% above its 200-day moving average; RSI is in the mid-60s; short interest is rising. The last three prints produced after-hours declines despite beats — a tell that expectations have caught up with execution. If the Q3 FY27 net new ARR figure merely meets rather than accelerates, the "sell the news" pattern could turn into a 20–30% de-rating with no fundamental change. Combine with the sales-leadership transition (Green replacing McCarthy in Feb 2026) and the risk of a single soft quarter is not trivial. This is a positioning and valuation risk, not a business risk — which is precisely why we prefer to accumulate below $90 rather than at the highs.

Glossary & Methodology

▾

Subscription ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)

The annualized value of all active subscription contracts at period end, assuming contracts expiring within 12 months renew on existing terms. Net new ARR is the quarter-over-quarter change (new logos + expansion − churn/contraction).

Net new ARR = ARR(t) − ARR(t−1)

Why it matters: ARR leads GAAP revenue by 2–4 quarters for ratable SaaS; it is the truest measure of demand. ↩ Back to Section 1 · ↩ Section 10

Free Cash Flow (FCF)

Cash from operations minus capital expenditures and capitalized software. Rubrik's definition: operating cash flow − purchases of property & equipment − capitalized internal-use software.

FCF = Operating Cash Flow − CapEx − Capitalized Software

Why it matters: Cash the business can actually deploy — but note it adds back non-cash stock compensation. ↩ Section 1 · ↩ Section 4

FCF Margin

FCF Margin = FCF ÷ Total Revenue

Why it matters: Shows how much of each revenue dollar becomes deployable cash; the key input to the "Rule of 40" (growth % + FCF margin % ≥ 40). Rubrik: 38% + 15% = 53 in Q2 FY27. ↩ Section 4

FCF per Share

FCF/Share = FCF ÷ Diluted Shares Outstanding

Why it matters: Adjusts cash generation for dilution; if shares grow 6.6%/yr, FCF must grow faster than that for owners to benefit. ↩ Section 4

Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)

Equity (RSUs, options, ESPP) granted to employees, expensed under GAAP but excluded from "non-GAAP" profit and added back in operating cash flow.

Owner earnings ≈ FCF − SBC

Why it matters: SBC is a real cost paid by shareholders through dilution. When SBC > FCF, "free cash flow positive" overstates economic profitability. ↩ Section 1 · ↩ Section 7

Subscription ARR Contribution Margin

Rubrik's proprietary leverage metric: period-end subscription ARR less the trailing-12-month non-GAAP subscription cost of revenue and non-GAAP operating expenses, divided by subscription ARR.

ARR CM = (Sub ARR − TTM non-GAAP sub COGS − TTM non-GAAP OpEx) ÷ Sub ARR

Why it matters: Shows what margin the current ARR base would earn on the current cost base — a forward-looking view of operating leverage. Caveat: it excludes SBC. ↩ Section 4

Price-to-Sales (P/S)

P/S = Market Cap ÷ TTM Revenue

Why it matters: The primary multiple for high-growth, low-profit companies. Our scale: >30 Euphoric · >18 Excited · >8 Optimistic · 4–8 Aware · <4 Cautious · <2 Pessimistic. Rubrik at 14.1× = Optimistic. ↩ Section 2 · ↩ Section 9

Price-to-Earnings (P/E)

P/E = Share Price ÷ Earnings per Share

Why it matters: The classic valuation yardstick; not meaningful (n/m) when earnings are negative or barely positive, as with RBRK on GAAP. We use non-GAAP forward P/E only as a cross-check. ↩ Section 2

Enterprise Value / FCF

EV = Market Cap + Debt − Cash; EV/FCF = EV ÷ TTM FCF

Why it matters: Capital-structure-neutral price for cash generation — better than P/E for companies with converts and large cash piles. Rubrik ~74× trailing, ~65× on FY27 guidance. ↩ Section 2 · ↩ Section 9

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)

CAGR = (End Value ÷ Start Value)^(1/years) − 1

Why it matters: Smooths lumpy growth into a single annual rate; used here for revenue (FY22–FY27E ≈ 27%) and market sizing. ↩ Section 2 · ↩ Section 4

TAM (Total Addressable Market)

The total annual revenue opportunity if a company captured 100% of its target market. Third-party TAM estimates vary widely by definition; we cite ranges and treat them as directional. ↩ Section 2

Probability-Weighted Price Target

PW Target = Σ (Probability × Scenario Target)

Why it matters: Forces explicit assumptions about the distribution of outcomes rather than a single point estimate. Our 1-year: 0.25×$162 + 0.50×$120 + 0.25×$70 = $118. ↩ Section 6
