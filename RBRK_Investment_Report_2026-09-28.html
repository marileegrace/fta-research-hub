Why this is mispriced

  • The 2026 selloff was about AI disruption, and it had the logic backwards. The stock fell to $42.25 in May on fears that AI could automate data recovery away. Our view is the opposite. As frontier models (Anthropic's Mythos class) make attacks faster and cheaper to run, prevention fails more often, so an immutable, clean recovery point becomes more valuable. Rubrik is on the recovery side of that trade.
  • Agent Cloud is a new product category, not a feature. Rubrik has 15+ paying Rubrik Agent Cloud customers. Agent Rewind reverses destructive agent actions, and SAGE is its intent-based guardrail engine. Both apply the "undo" primitive Rubrik already owns to AI agents. There is an integration with Anthropic's Claude Code, and a GSI alliance (Project Hourglass: Cognizant, Deloitte, HCLTech, NTT DATA, Wipro) to sell it.
  • Operating leverage is showing up fast. Subscription ARR contribution margin rose from 8.0% to 14.0% in five quarters, with ~15.5% guided for FY27. G&A fell from 21.5% to 13.0% of revenue YoY.
  • The headline revenue deceleration is mostly an accounting effect. Q3 guidance implies only 23% reported growth. That is because one-time material-rights revenue is rolling off. Normalized growth is 31%. Investors who screen on headline growth may sell into the print, which could create a buying opportunity.
What would make us wrong: (1) Net new ARR falls below ~$80M for two quarters in a row, (2) NRR drops under 115%, or (3) SBC plus dilution stays above ~6% a year while FCF margin stalls under 20%. Any one of these would mean the platform story is not converting into per-share value.

Microeconomic & Valuation Analysis

▾

Industry & TAM

Rubrik's core market is data replication and protection software. IDC put vendor sales at about $12.3B in 2024, and third-party estimates of recovery-solution growth run as high as ~19% CAGR (a single vendor-published estimate; treat it as an upper bound). Cohesity, the largest private competitor after merging with Veritas, defines a broader $40B+ TAM across cyber resilience. Rubrik is building three more layers on top of that core: identity resilience (Active Directory/Entra/Okta recovery, extended by the Strata.io acquisition), SaaS and cloud-native protection (M365, Google Workspace, Apache Iceberg on AWS), and AI agent operations (Agent Cloud). We estimate these take the addressable market above $50B by 2030. Our model assumes the agent layer alone could be a $5–10B market by then, which is speculative.

Competitive landscape

By market share, Cohesity-Veritas is #1 (~$1.6B ARR, mid-teens growth, IPO expected but no S-1 yet) and Veeam is #2 and gaining. Rubrik is growing faster than either, at 33% ARR growth vs. roughly 15% for Cohesity. Legacy vendors (Dell, IBM, Commvault) are losing share. Commvault (CVLT) is the only listed pure-play peer. It grows ~11% and trades at 5x sales.

Qualitative Analysis: Moat, Management & Culture

▾

The moat, from first principles

Backup software has historically been a commodity: every vendor copies data somewhere else. Rubrik's moat comes from a different design choice. It was built from day one around an immutable, metadata-rich index of an enterprise's entire data estate. Three effects follow:

  • Switching costs compound over time. Each year of immutable snapshots, retention policies and compliance history is harder to migrate. Customers do not move their last line of defense in a hurry. NRR above 119% and 88% of ARR coming from $100K+ customers are consistent with that.
  • A data-gravity flywheel. Because Rubrik already indexes the data, it can add threat detection, sensitive-data monitoring, identity recovery and now agent rollback with little extra cost of goods sold. Each module makes the index more valuable, and a more valuable index sells more modules.
  • Trust at the worst possible moment. Recovery is used rarely, but when it is needed it is mission-critical. A customer who has recovered once after ransomware tends to stay. This is a reputational moat: slow to build and quick to lose after a single public recovery failure.

A systems view: as AI agents take autonomous actions inside enterprises, the question moves from "who is allowed to act?" to "how do we reverse what was done?" Identity vendors (Okta, CyberArk inside PANW) own the first question. Rubrik is well placed to own the second, and we think it is the more durable position because no preventive control reaches 100%.

Founder-led? Yes

Co-founder Bipul Sinha is CEO and Chairman. He is a former Lightspeed partner and an early Nutanix investor, and has led Rubrik since 2014. His record: taking Rubrik from appliance sales to a subscription/cloud model (FY23–FY25), and now positioning it for agentic AI ahead of peers. Co-founder Arvind Jain left years ago and founded Glean. CFO Kiran Choudary has delivered 10 straight quarters of guide-and-beat. CRO Jesse Green (ex-MongoDB, AppDynamics) was promoted in Feb-2026 when Brian McCarthy departed. The two quarters since the transition have been the company's strongest net new ARR prints, which suggests the handoff went well.

Capital allocation

  • Issued a ~$1.15B 0% convertible note due 2030 in mid-2025, with capped calls. The terms are cheap, but ~9.2M potentially dilutive shares sit behind it.
  • Small tuck-in acquisitions (Laminar for DSPM, Predibase for AI, Strata.io for identity orchestration). This is disciplined and focused on capabilities. Management put zero ARR from Strata into guidance.
  • A £375M ($500M) UK investment, with London named as EMEA HQ. International revenue grew +52% YoY.
  • The weak spot is SBC. SBC ran ~$342M TTM, about 22% of revenue, which exceeds FCF. There is no buyback program to offset it.

Risks & Anti-Thesis: Steelmanning the Bear Case

▾

If we were short RBRK, these are the five arguments we would make.

1. "The FCF is an illusion paid for in stock"

Probability: High (70%)Impact: Medium-HighPriced in: Partially

TTM SBC ($342M) exceeds TTM FCF (~$287M). Owners are being diluted ~5.7% a year, and Q2 SBC jumped to $101M. On an owner-earnings basis Rubrik has never made money. If investors start valuing FCF net of SBC, as they did with SNOW and ZS in past cycles, the multiple could compress sharply.

2. "Headline growth is about to fall off a cliff"

Probability: Certain (optics)Impact: MediumPriced in: Partially

Q3 revenue guide implies +23% reported growth, down from +38% in Q2, as material-rights revenue runs off. Quant screens and generalists will see a halving of growth. Adjusted net new Cloud ARR grew only 20% YoY. Stripping out migrations, the true new-business engine is growing slower than the headline 39%.

3. "Cohesity and hyperscalers squeeze the core"

Probability: Medium (40%)Impact: HighPriced in: No

Cohesity-Veritas is bigger (~$1.6B ARR), profitable (28% adjusted margin) and plans to IPO. A public Cohesity trading at a lower multiple would give the market a cheaper comp, and it would have cash to fight on price. AWS, Azure and Google keep improving native backup. In a downturn, "good enough and bundled" often wins.

4. "Agent Cloud is a narrative, not a business"

Probability: Medium (45%)Impact: MediumPriced in: Partially

15 paying customers is small next to 3,084 $100K+ customers. Agent security is crowded: Palo Alto (with CyberArk), CrowdStrike, Microsoft, Okta and dozens of startups. If AI agent governance becomes a feature of identity or endpoint platforms, Rubrik's premium AI multiple disappears.

5. "Governance and insider supply"

Probability: Medium (50%)Impact: Low-MediumPriced in: Mostly

Dual-class shares limit outside influence. Steady 10b5-1 selling plus the CEO's new $42M prepaid forward create a constant overhang. The 0% convertible (2030) adds ~9M shares if converted. A founder-centric culture concentrates key-person risk in Bipul Sinha.

Our rebuttal in one line: risks 1 and 2 are real but already known and partly priced (the stock trades at a third of CRWD's P/S despite faster ARR growth). Risk 3 is the one that could break the thesis, so watch the Cohesity S-1 closely.

Glossary & Methodology

▾
Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) / Subscription ARR
The annualized value of all active subscription contracts at a point in time. ARR = Σ active contract value, annualized
Why it matters: it is a better measure of a subscription business's real momentum than GAAP revenue, which can be distorted by accounting items like material rights. ↩ back
Net Revenue Retention (NRR) / Dollar-Based Net Retention
How much ARR last year's customers generate today, after upsells minus churn and downgrades. NRR = ARR today from year-ago customers ÷ their ARR a year ago
Why it matters: above 110% means the company grows even with zero new customers. Rubrik reports a 4-quarter average. ↩ back
Free Cash Flow (FCF) & FCF Margin
Cash left over after running the business and investing in equipment and software. FCF = Operating Cash Flow − CapEx − Capitalized Software FCF Margin = FCF ÷ Revenue
Why it matters: it is the cash available to reinvest, repay debt or return to shareholders. It is harder to manipulate than earnings. ↩ back
Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)
Employee pay in shares or options instead of cash. It is a real cost to shareholders (via dilution) but is added back in cash-flow statements. SBC-adjusted FCF = FCF − SBC
Why it matters: high SBC can make FCF look better than the economic reality. ↩ back
Price-to-Sales (P/S) & FTA Sentiment Scale
P/S = Market Cap ÷ TTM Revenue
FTA scale: >30 Euphoric · >18 Excited · 8–18 Optimistic · 4–8 Aware · <4 Cautious · <2 Pessimistic.
Why it matters: it is the best available valuation tool for companies that are not yet GAAP-profitable. ↩ back
Price-to-Earnings (P/E)
P/E = Price ÷ EPS. It is not meaningful when EPS is negative. Forward P/E uses projected (often non-GAAP) EPS.
Why it matters: it tells you how many years of current earnings you are paying for. ↩ back
Enterprise Value / ARR (EV/ARR)
EV = Market Cap + Debt − Cash EV/ARR = EV ÷ Subscription ARR
Why it matters: it adjusts for the balance sheet and uses the forward-looking recurring base. It is the standard SaaS valuation metric. ↩ back
EV/EBITDA
EV ÷ Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortization
Why it matters: it compares companies regardless of capital structure. It is distorted for high-SBC software companies because GAAP EBITDA includes SBC. ↩ back
Subscription ARR Contribution Margin
A Rubrik-defined metric. (Sub. ARR − TTM non-GAAP sub. COGS − TTM non-GAAP opex) ÷ Sub. ARR
Why it matters: it shows how much profit the recurring base would produce if the company stopped investing in growth, so it is a measure of operating leverage. ↩ back
Material Rights (revenue)
An accounting (ASC 606) treatment. When customers received rights to convert legacy licenses to cloud subscriptions, part of the original deal value was deferred and is recognized later as revenue.
Why it matters: it inflated Rubrik's reported revenue in FY26 and is now rolling off. That makes reported growth (Q3 guide +23%) look worse than underlying growth (+31%). ↩ back
Convertible Senior Notes
Debt that can be converted into shares at a preset price. Rubrik's are 0% coupon, due 2030, with "capped calls" bought to limit dilution.
Why it matters: it is cheap funding, but it carries potential future dilution (~9.2M shares). ↩ back
Share Dilution
Dilution % = (Shares now − Shares a year ago) ÷ Shares a year ago
Why it matters: each existing share owns a smaller slice of the company, so per-share metrics must grow faster than the share count. ↩ back
Current Ratio
Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
Why it matters: it measures short-term liquidity. For SaaS, deferred revenue inflates liabilities, so ratios near 1.5x are healthy. ↩ back
Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)
CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1/years) − 1
Why it matters: it smooths volatile year-to-year growth into one comparable rate. ↩ back
Operating Leverage
When revenue grows faster than operating costs, margins expand. Degree ≈ %Δ Operating Income ÷ %Δ Revenue
Why it matters: it is the mechanism that turns a growth company into a cash compounder. ↩ back
Non-GAAP Metrics
Company-adjusted figures that exclude SBC, amortization and one-time items.
Why it matters: they are useful for seeing core operations, but they always flatter results. Compare them with GAAP. ↩ back
Economic Moat
A durable competitive advantage (switching costs, network effects, scale, brand or data) that protects returns from competitors.
Why it matters: without a moat, high returns attract competition until they disappear. ↩ back
Dual-Class Share Structure
Class B shares (held by founders and early insiders) carry more votes than the public Class A shares.
Why it matters: it protects long-term vision but weakens outside shareholders' governance influence. ↩ back
Prepaid Variable Forward (PVF)
A contract where an insider pledges shares to a bank, receives cash upfront (typically 75–90% of value), and settles later in shares or cash depending on the future price.
Why it matters: it provides liquidity and hedges downside without an immediate sale. It still signals reduced net exposure. ↩ back
Total Addressable Market (TAM)
Total annual revenue available if a company captured 100% of its market.
Why it matters: it sets the ceiling on long-run growth. ↩ back
Rule of 40
Growth % + FCF Margin % ≥ 40
Why it matters: it is a quick test of whether a software company balances growth and profitability well. Rubrik scores ~52. ↩ back
