Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

▾
Thesis (Bull): Rubrik is transitioning from "backup vendor" to the recovery-and-governance layer for the AI enterprise, and the numbers already show it: net new subscription ARR re-accelerated to +35% YoY in Q2 FY27 with zero inorganic help, free cash flow guidance has been raised twice this year to $323–333M, and the subscription ARR contribution margin has expanded 460 bps in twelve months. At ~10x forward sales for a business compounding ARR near 30% with an 18–19% FCF margin, the stock is priced like a mature data-protection name, not like the fastest-growing scaled platform in cyber resilience. We assign a 1-year probability-weighted target of $107 (+20%) and a 3-year target of $140 (+57%).

Rubrik reported Q2 FY27 (quarter ended July 31, 2026) on August 27. Every guided metric was beaten for the 10th consecutive quarter as a public company. Revenue grew 38% to $427.3M (43% normalized for material rights), subscription ARR reached $1.66B, non-GAAP EPS was $0.20 against a $0.03–0.05 guide, and FCF was $65.7M. Management raised full-year guidance across the board. The stock nonetheless fell ~13% the next day and now sits ~17% below its August 27 high of $107.91.

Why the drop? Three surface-level concerns: non-GAAP gross margin ticked down to 81.0% (from 81.6%), the "cloud" flavor of net new ARR grew only 20% on an adjusted basis because the legacy-to-cloud migration tailwind is ending, and the implied second-half net new ARR guide steps down to mid-single-digit growth. We address each in Section 7. Our view is that they are second-order effects of a business that is deliberately growing its sovereign / self-hosted segment (which carries higher margins), and of a management team that has under-promised by a wide margin eight quarters in a row.

What the market is missing

  • The forward guide is a floor, not a forecast. Rubrik's FY27 subscription ARR guide has moved from $1,829–1,839M (March) to $1,854–1,862M (June) to $1,880–1,885M (August). The initial FY26 guide of $1,350–1,360M ended at $1,461M — a 7.7% beat on a metric that is nearly impossible to "manage." The 2H implied deceleration is the same pattern as last year.
  • Operating leverage is compounding. Non-GAAP operating income swung from –$4.4M to +$33.5M YoY in Q2, on a 38% revenue base increase. Non-GAAP S&M fell from 49% of revenue to 44%; G&A from 12% to 8%.
  • Identity Resilience and Rubrik Agent Cloud are optionality that is not in the guide. Management explicitly excludes RAC and the Strata acquisition from FY27 ARR guidance. RAC has >15 paying customers; Identity is "one of the fastest-growing product lines in company history."
  • Balance sheet is a net-cash position. $1.75B cash and short-term investments vs. $1.13B of 0%-coupon convertible notes; deferred revenue of $1.93B is a cushion of future recognized revenue.

Microeconomic & Valuation Analysis

▾

Industry & TAM

Rubrik plays in three overlapping markets. The core is backup & recovery / cyber resilience, which third-party estimates size at roughly $16–19B in 2026, growing 10–14% annually as ransomware forces a shift from "prevention" budgets to "recovery" budgets. Layered on top is data security posture and identity resilience (DSPM, identity recovery, Active Directory / Entra / Okta protection), and the newest layer is AI-agent security and governance (Rubrik Agent Cloud), a market that barely existed 18 months ago. In its IPO filing, Rubrik framed the combined data-security addressable market at roughly $53B by 2027; the AI-agent layer is incremental to that.

The structural driver is simple: enterprises have spent two decades funding detection and prevention (endpoint, network, identity), and those layers are now being outpaced by AI-driven attacks that chain vulnerabilities at machine speed. When prevention fails, the only variable that matters is time-to-clean-recovery. That is Rubrik's core competency, and it explains why the company has been named a Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader for seven consecutive years, positioned furthest on "Vision" in 2026.

GAAP P/E is meaningless here because Rubrik expenses ~$100M per quarter of stock-based compensation. The correct lens is price-to-free-cash-flow and EV/Sales relative to growth. On P/S, Rubrik's 11.9x trailing multiple lands in the "Optimistic" band (8–18x) of our scale. That is not cheap, but it is roughly half the multiple of the only faster-growing scaled peer at similar margin structure (CrowdStrike ~40x) and 2.5x the multiple of the legacy incumbent (Commvault ~4.5x) that is growing at half Rubrik's rate. A Rule-of-40 score of ~52 (33% ARR growth + ~19% FCF margin) supports a premium multiple.

Institutional ownership & insider activity

Institutions own approximately 69% of shares outstanding; insiders own ~18% (largely founder/VC-era holdings including Class B super-voting shares held by CEO Bipul Sinha and early backers Lightspeed and Greylock). Short interest is 7.2% of shares (9.1% of float, 5.3 days to cover) — elevated, up from 13.7M to 15.0M shares month-over-month, indicating some accumulation of a bear position post-earnings that could fuel a squeeze on a Q3 beat.

Insider activity is the least attractive part of the picture: across the past six months, insiders executed 139 open-market sales and zero purchases (many under 10b5-1 plans). CEO Sinha sold ~$46.5M of Class A stock in September 2025; CTO Arvind Nithrakashyap sold ~$3.0M in August 2026. This is typical for a post-IPO founder cohort monetizing after a decade of illiquidity, and Sinha retains substantial economic and voting control, but it is not a bullish signal and we weight it in the conviction score.

Analyst growth estimates

Consensus (28 analysts) rates RBRK "Strong Buy" with a $118.70 average target (+33%). Post-Q2 targets clustered at $114–130: Citi $130, BTIG $125, Wedbush $120 (added to "Best Ideas"), CIBC $120, Scotiabank $120, DA Davidson $115, BMO $115, Rosenblatt $115, Piper $114. The three-year consensus revenue growth forecast is ~23.5% CAGR, which we view as conservative given subscription ARR is still growing 33% and revenue normalized for material rights is guided to outpace ARR growth.

Qualitative Analysis — Moat, Management & Culture

▾

Competitive moat — a systems view

Backup is one of the stickiest workloads in enterprise IT, but stickiness alone is not a moat (Commvault and Veritas were sticky for 20 years and still lost share). Rubrik's moat comes from a specific architectural choice made years ago: it indexes both the time-series of every backup and the metadata/application layer (identity, configuration, dependencies). That gives the company a continuously updated, immutable, air-gapped map of the enterprise's "last known good state." Three consequences flow from that:

  • Preemptive recovery. Rubrik pre-computes clean recovery points in peacetime so it can restore in hours rather than days in wartime. Competitors that only store bytes cannot answer "which snapshot is clean?" without a forensic project.
  • Complementary network effects. Each additional product (M365, cloud, unstructured data, identity, agent cloud) plugs into the same policy engine and makes the existing products more valuable — customers increasingly buy via "Rubrik Flex," a single platform contract. NRR above 119% for a decade-old cohort base is the evidence.
  • Data gravity. Once petabytes of immutable backups sit in Rubrik's format, switching costs are measured in years and legal risk. Churn is structurally low.

Competitive set: Cohesity-Veritas (private, largest by revenue after the merger, integration risk), Commvault (public, 22% ARR growth, slower SaaS transition), Veeam (private, SMB/mid-market strength), hyperscaler-native backup (AWS Backup, Azure Backup — "good enough" for single-cloud shops), and in the AI-agent layer, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Okta, and startups. Rubrik's win rates against legacy vendors remain "extremely high" per management, with two named Global 2000 displacements this quarter.

Founder-led? Yes.

Bipul Sinha (CEO, Chairman, co-founder, ex-Lightspeed partner) has run the company since 2014 with co-founder Arvind Nithrakashyap as CTO. Founder-led companies with technical co-founders still in the building tend to sustain innovation pace longer; Rubrik's cadence — Agent Cloud (Oct 2025), SAGE, Agent Rewind, Agent Identity, Identity Roll Forward, Annapurna, Iceberg protection, Google Workspace protection — all within 12 months — is consistent with that pattern.

Executive bench & capital allocation

CFO Kiran Choudary (ex-Cohesity) has established a clear beat-and-raise discipline since the IPO. The June 2025 $1.15B 0% convertible raise refinanced expensive debt ($330M) and, with capped calls, pushed dilution risk to well above the current price. The company promoted Jesse Green (ex-President, Americas) to CRO in March 2026 — a "leadership transition in the global revenue organization" that the 10-K flags as a risk but that Q1 and Q2 execution suggests has been absorbed. Capital allocation is disciplined: tuck-in M&A (Predibase 2025, Strata Identity 2026 for ~$24M net cash) rather than transformational deals; no buybacks yet (SBC-driven dilution is the offset we watch).

Culture & organizational traits

  • Customer obsession: 3,084 customers above $100K ARR (+23%); customers above $1M ARR growing 57% and now 88% of ARR — the company is becoming a strategic vendor rather than a tool.
  • Innovation pace: ~$127M/quarter in GAAP R&D (30% of revenue), with a public roadmap that has anticipated the "assume breach, recover fast" doctrine by several years.
  • Failure tolerance / idea meritocracy: Rubrik participates in Anthropic's Project Glasswing (frontier AI vulnerability research) and openly reframes its own product category — evidence of a learning organization.
  • Talent density: 3,797 employees generating ~$406K revenue per head, rising every quarter — headcount growth is running well below revenue growth, which is the definition of operating leverage.
  • Ethics / governance watch-items: dual-class structure concentrates control; heavy insider selling; SBC at ~24% of revenue. None are disqualifying, all are worth monitoring.

Quantitative Analysis — Financial Deep Dive

▾

Revenue streams, explained simply

Rubrik sells one thing: a subscription to Rubrik Security Cloud (and its self-hosted sibling, RSC-Private). Subscription revenue (95% of total) is recognized ratably for cloud/SaaS and partly upfront for term licenses. Other revenue (5%) is professional services plus a small amount of hardware appliance sales in APAC regions not yet transitioned to partners. Within subscription, ~89% of ARR is Cloud ARR and ~11% is non-cloud (sovereign, government, regulated on-prem) — a segment that was shrinking during the migration years and is now growing ~10% again.

The flywheel

Land with one workload (typically M365 or on-prem VM backup) → immutable data accumulates → cross-sell adjacent workloads via the same policy engine (cloud, SaaS, unstructured, identity) → NRR >119% → larger customers ($1M+ cohort +57%) → platform contracts (Flex) → higher gross retention and lower cost-to-serve → margin expansion funds R&D into the next adjacency (Agent Cloud) → repeat. The Q2 data shows every stage of the loop turning.

Risks & Anti-Thesis — Steelmanning the Bear

▾

If we were short RBRK, these are the five arguments we would lead with, in order of strength.

1. The growth deceleration is real, and the migration tailwind masked it

Probability 40%Impact HighPriced in Partially

Subscription ARR growth has decelerated from 39% (Q4 FY25) to 33%. A meaningful chunk of "cloud ARR" growth over the past two years came from migrating existing on-prem customers to hosted products — a one-time uplift management now says is "wrapping up this year." Adjusted net new cloud ARR grew only 20% in Q2. The 2H FY27 guide implies net new ARR growth of mid-single digits. If that guide is accurate rather than conservative, the company is a 25%-grower being valued as a 33%-grower, and the multiple should compress toward 7–8x sales (~$65).

Our rebuttal: total net new ARR (which is what pays the bills) accelerated to +35% with zero inorganic contribution, and the non-cloud business flipped from shrinking to growing ~10%. The migration tailwind ending was disclosed a year ago. Management's 2H guide has been a floor for eight quarters. But this is the risk that would break the thesis fastest.

2. Stock-based compensation and dilution make the "FCF" partly illusory

Probability 70%Impact MediumPriced in Mostly

SBC was $101M in Q2 (23.6% of revenue) and ~$330M for FY26 (25% of revenue). Subtract SBC from TTM FCF of $286M and the business is roughly break-even on a "true" owner-earnings basis. Share count grows 6–7% per year, so a 20% stock return requires ~27% enterprise value growth. GAAP losses (–$254M TTM) are not going away soon, and the GAAP P/E will remain undefined for years. Insiders have sold 139 times in six months with zero buys — they know the SBC math.

Our rebuttal: SBC as a percentage of revenue has fallen from ~103% (FY25, IPO-related) to 25% (FY26) to ~22% (1H FY27), and the company began net-settling awards this year. At 30%+ growth the ratio will keep falling. But investors should value RBRK on FCF minus SBC-adjusted dilution, which is why we cap our multiple at 40x FCF in the base case rather than the 60x the stock trades at today.

3. Rubrik Agent Cloud is a story, not a business — and the story is crowded

Probability 50%Impact MediumPriced in Partially

RAC has "over 15 paying customers" and contributes negligible ARR. Meanwhile CrowdStrike (Charlotte Agentic SOAR), Palo Alto, Okta, Microsoft, and dozens of venture-backed startups are all positioning for agent governance. Identity vendors already own the access plane; observability vendors already own the telemetry. Rubrik's edge (Agent Rewind) is real but narrow, and the "AI-agent security" premium embedded in the multiple could deflate if RAC is still sub-$50M ARR in two years.

Our rebuttal: our base case assigns RAC essentially zero value; it is optionality. The core cyber-resilience business alone justifies the base-case target. If RAC works, it is upside; if it fails, it costs R&D dollars, not the thesis.

4. Gross-margin erosion from mix and hosting costs

Probability 35%Impact MediumPriced in Yes

Non-GAAP gross margin slipped from 83.7% (Q4 FY26) to 82.9% (Q1) to 81.0% (Q2). Management's long-term band is 77–82%, meaning they are guiding to potential further compression. Drivers: lower high-margin material-rights revenue, growth in low-margin "other" (hardware in APAC, up 56% YoY), and cloud hosting costs that scale with data volume. If GM settles at 78%, the FCF margin ceiling drops by ~300 bps and the base-case target falls ~10%.

Our rebuttal: the non-cloud (sovereign/self-hosted) business that is growing again carries higher gross margins because Rubrik does not host it. Hardware revenue is noise (5% of total). The August 28 selloff already priced this risk.

5. Hyperscaler-native backup and a merged Cohesity-Veritas compress pricing

Probability 30%Impact HighPriced in No

AWS Backup, Azure Backup, and Google's equivalents are "free-ish" for single-cloud workloads and improving every quarter. Cohesity-Veritas, now the largest vendor by revenue with a private-equity balance sheet, can compete aggressively on price in the enterprise. Commvault is closing the SaaS gap (38% SaaS ARR growth). Rubrik's 119% NRR depends on expanding wallet share; if per-TB pricing falls 20%, NRR falls toward 110% and the growth algorithm changes.

Our rebuttal: hyperscaler tools do not solve multi-cloud, SaaS, identity, or clean-point recovery; Rubrik's displacement wins this quarter came against cloud-native tools. But pricing power is the key variable to watch in NRR.

Other risks noted
Dual-class governance and founder control; CRO transition (Jesse Green, March 2026); $1.15B convert matures 2030 and would need refinancing at a real coupon if the stock is below the conversion price; macro-driven security budget compression; a major recovery failure at a marquee customer would damage the brand disproportionately.

Glossary & Methodology

▾

Methodology

All financial figures come from Rubrik's SEC-filed 8-K earnings releases (Q3 FY26 through Q2 FY27, plus prior-year comparatives), the Q2 FY27 earnings call transcript, and market data from StockAnalysis / S&P Global as of the September 10, 2026 close. Peer data for ZS, S and VRNS came from secondary sources and is marked approximate. Scenario targets apply EV/FCF and EV/Sales multiples to FTA's own revenue and margin model; probabilities are judgmental. Nothing here is investment advice.

Subscription ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)

ARR = annualized value of active subscription contracts at period end

Rubrik's headline metric. Net new ARR = ARR this quarter − ARR last quarter, capturing new logos and expansion net of churn. It leads revenue because subscription revenue is recognized ratably after the contract is signed. Why it matters: it is the cleanest real-time read on demand.

↑ Back to Section 1

Cloud ARR

Subset of Subscription ARR from hosted (RSC / RSC-Gov) products

Excludes self-hosted RSC-Private. "Adjusted net new Cloud ARR" strips out legacy customers migrating to hosted products so investors can see organic cloud demand. Why it matters: the migration tailwind is ending, so this adjusted figure (+20%) is the "clean" cloud growth rate.

↑ Back to Section 4

Net Retention Rate (NRR)

NRR = ARR today from customers who existed 12 months ago ÷ their ARR 12 months ago

Above 100% means the existing base grows on its own through upsell net of churn. Rubrik reports a four-quarter average. Why it matters: at 119%, Rubrik would grow ~19% with zero new customers.

↑ Back to Section 3

Free Cash Flow (FCF)

FCF = Operating cash flow − capex − capitalized internal-use software

Cash the business generates after reinvestment; FCF margin = FCF ÷ revenue. Rubrik's definition excludes acquisitions. Why it matters: for a GAAP-loss company, FCF is the only cash-based profitability measure — but it flatters companies with high stock-based compensation (see SBC).

↑ Back to Section 4

Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)

Non-cash expense for equity granted to employees; added back in FCF and non-GAAP earnings

Real cost borne by shareholders through dilution rather than cash. Why it matters: Rubrik's SBC (~22–25% of revenue) is the gap between its 18% FCF margin and its –16% GAAP net margin.

↑ Back to Section 7

Subscription ARR Contribution Margin

(Sub ARR − TTM non-GAAP subscription COGS − TTM non-GAAP opex) ÷ Sub ARR

A Rubrik-specific leverage metric that compares the forward-looking revenue base (ARR) to the trailing cost base. Why it matters: it moves before GAAP margins do and is what management guides to.

↑ Back to Section 4

Material Rights Revenue

Revenue recognized when a customer exercises a discounted option embedded in a prior contract

Arose from Rubrik's cloud-transition contracts; $18M expected in FY27 vs far more in FY26. Why it matters: it inflated prior-year revenue growth, so "normalized" growth (43% in Q2) is the truer comparison.

↑ Back to Section 1

Price-to-Sales (P/S) and the FTA Scale

P/S = Market cap ÷ TTM revenue · Scale: >30 Euphoric · >18 Excited · >8 Optimistic · 4–8 Aware · <4 Cautious · <2 Pessimistic

Useful for unprofitable growth companies where P/E fails. Why it matters: RBRK at 11.9x is "Optimistic" — a premium that must be earned by sustained growth.

↑ Back to Section 2

Price-to-Free-Cash-Flow (P/FCF)

P/FCF = Market cap ÷ TTM free cash flow (EV/FCF uses enterprise value)

The cash-flow analogue of P/E. Why it matters: RBRK at ~60x is expensive on today's cash but reasonable if FCF compounds 40%+ for three years.

↑ Back to Section 2

EV / EBITDA

Enterprise value ÷ earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation & amortization

Capital-structure-neutral profitability multiple. Why it matters: not meaningful for RBRK today (negative EBITDA on a GAAP basis) — another reason to use FCF.

↑ Back to Section 9

Rule of 40

Revenue (or ARR) growth % + FCF margin % ≥ 40

A SaaS heuristic for balancing growth and profitability. Why it matters: RBRK scores ~52, which justifies a premium P/S.

↑ Back to Section 2

Operating Leverage

% change in operating income ÷ % change in revenue

When costs grow slower than revenue, margins expand. Why it matters: Rubrik's non-GAAP operating income went from –$4M to +$33M on 38% revenue growth — extreme leverage.

↑ Back to Section 3

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)

CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1/years) − 1

Smoothed annual growth across multi-year periods. Why it matters: FY22–FY26 revenue CAGR of 27% vs ~12% for the industry.

↑ Back to Section 4

Share Dilution

YoY % change in shares outstanding

New shares from SBC, ESPP, option exercises, or convertibles reduce each existing share's claim on the business. Why it matters: at +6.6%/yr, RBRK must grow enterprise value ~7% just to keep the share price flat.

↑ Back to Section 4

Current Ratio / Working Capital

Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities · Working capital = the difference

Short-term liquidity. Why it matters: 1.64x is healthy, and much of RBRK's current liabilities is deferred revenue (cash already collected), not debt.

↑ Back to Section 4

Altman Z-Score

Weighted blend of working capital, retained earnings, EBIT, equity value and sales vs. total assets/liabilities

A 1968 bankruptcy predictor calibrated on industrial companies. Why it matters: it flags RBRK (1.49) because of the accumulated deficit and negative equity; it ignores the $1.75B cash pile and positive FCF, so we discount it.

↑ Back to Section 4

Convertible Senior Notes

Debt that can convert into equity at a preset price; 0% coupon here, with capped calls to limit dilution

Rubrik issued $1.15B in June 2025 to retire higher-cost debt. Why it matters: near-free financing today, but a real refinancing question in 2030 if the stock is below the conversion price.

↑ Back to Section 1

Short Interest

Shares sold short ÷ float (or shares outstanding); days to cover = short shares ÷ average daily volume

Measures bearish positioning. Why it matters: 9.1% of float and 5.3 days to cover means a strong Q3 print could force short covering and amplify upside.

↑ Back to Section 2

TAM (Total Addressable Market)

Annual revenue opportunity if a company captured 100% of its target market

Vendor-stated TAMs (Rubrik: ~$53B) are typically far larger than third-party segment estimates ($16–19B for backup & recovery). Why it matters: use the narrow figure for penetration math and the broad one for optionality.
