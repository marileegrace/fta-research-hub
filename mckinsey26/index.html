The One-Page Read

Thesis: The report ranks highest on both investment and enterprise adoption for exactly three areas — energy & data-center infrastructure, cyber resilience, and inference-era compute — and is most cautious on the pure-software AI categories where public-market multiples are richest. The trade is to own the constraints (power, memory/packaging, recovery, launch) rather than the narratives (agents, humanoids, quantum).

How to read the multiples
The 12× jumps in agentic software development (Cursor, $60B) and space (SpaceX IPO) are single megadeals, not broad waves — McKinsey says so itself. The 5.3× on AI infrastructure, the 2.1× on robotics, and the 3.2× on AI-for-science are the durable signals.

Five Themes That Should Move Your Positioning

1. The bottleneck is power, not chips

US AI data centers will consume as much electricity as California by 2030. Transformer lead times exceed two years; 2,500 GW of projects sit in grid queues. Hyperscalers have moved from buying power to orchestrating it: Google–Kairos–TVA advanced nuclear, AWS–Talen 1,920 MW from Susquehanna, Microsoft–Chevron a dedicated 2.67 GW gas plant. McKinsey's own phrase: "time to energy" now matters as much as cost.

Action · Favor anything that shortens time-to-energy: grid equipment and transformers, behind-the-meter generation, clean-firm power (nuclear, geothermal), storage, and data-center thermal/power gear. Existing research this validates: VST (merchant nuclear/gas generation — the purest "time to energy" play), BE (on-site fuel cells that skip the interconnection queue — the report's "behind-the-meter" bridge, though gas-fed and outside its "clean firm" bucket), VRT (power & thermal), ET (gas midstream to data centers), FSLR (domestic solar), and the power-to-compute converters IREN, WULF, CORZ, NBIS. Queue names it supports: NXT, RUN, APLD.

2. Inference is overtaking training — custom silicon is the weapon

Inference will be 30–40% of all data-center demand by 2030 and the majority of AI compute. Hyperscalers are turning internal chips (Google TPU, AWS Trainium/Inferentia) into customer-facing products; Anthropic plans up to one million TPUs. McKinsey names HBM, advanced packaging, high-speed interconnect, and optical networking as the strategic differentiators, and flags memory/packaging capacity as a possible cap on the whole build-out. Cerebras filed for IPO.

Action · The inference era broadens the winners beyond the GPU leader. Memory, packaging, optical/networking and CPUs-for-orchestration are the under-owned layers. Researched names: AMD (CPUs for orchestration + inference accelerators), CRDO (high-speed interconnect), TER (test for advanced packaging), AMBA (edge inference silicon), and the neoclouds that monetize the chips: NBIS, CORZ, IREN, WULF. Coverage gap to fill: HBM/memory and optical interconnect.

3. The cyber defense window has collapsed

More than three-quarters of vulnerabilities are zero-day; exploitation of public-facing apps rose 44% in a year and active ransomware groups rose 49% (IBM X-Force). Frontier models (Claude Mythos / Project Glasswing, Z.ai's GLM-5.2) find flaws faster for both sides. McKinsey's prescription is continuous runtime visibility, automated containment, agentic security operations, identity, and post-quantum migration — i.e., assume breach, recover at machine speed.

Action · Direct validation of the RBRK thesis published 2026-09-10 (recovery + identity + agent governance), and of the CBRS report. Platform-trust angle also touches NOW and ORCL (sovereign/secure enterprise stacks). Also supports platform consolidators: CRWD, PANW (Portkey acquisition for AI runtime security). Watch the concentration risk the report flags — value accrues to a few platforms.

4. Physical AI is arriving — manufacturing and logistics first

Robotics funding hit a record $19.5B and is on pace to double. Vision-language-action models and world models (Google Gemini Robotics, Physical Intelligence, Open X-Embodiment) are turning robots into adaptable systems. Humanoids are shipping in volume from China (Agibot, Ubtech) but deployments are still pilots; Siemens and BMW are testing wheeled humanoids instead. Robotaxis are repeatable services now (Waymo 20M+ rides, Baidu 20M+), with a UNECE global framework adopted June 2026.

Action · McKinsey explicitly asks whether value accrues to hardware or to "models, simulation, data, compute, fleet orchestration." Favor picks-and-shovels — sensors, robotics processors, simulation, test — over humanoid makers. Researched: TSLA (Optimus + robotaxi — the report's humanoid-ROI caution applies directly), AMBA and AMD (robotics/edge compute), TER (robotics test/automation). Queue: OUST, ZBRA, NOVT, ALNT, UBER.

5. Space went mainstream

SpaceX raised $85.7B in its June IPO; Amazon Leo has launched 300+ satellites; LEO broadband is mainstream; direct-to-device is operational (AST SpaceMobile BlueBird, including the BlueBird 7 loss as a reminder of deployment risk); defense demand (Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture) is the backbone; orbital compute is in early experimentation. Key uncertainty: whether demand for launched mass keeps pace with super-heavy supply, and whether D2D profit pools are durable.

Action · Supports the researched space cluster: ASTS (named in the report as the D2D bellwether, BlueBird 7 loss included), RKLB (launch cadence + defense), SATS (spectrum/D2D). Queue: FLY, PL, RDW, BKSY, SATL. Weight defense-exposed and Earth-observation-analytics names over pure launch bets until cadence is proven.

Where McKinsey Is Telling You to Be Careful

Agentic AI / agentic coding: most hyped, least monetized. Only one in four companies using agentic dev tools show meaningful lifecycle acceleration; "token spend" is emerging as a cost line; governance, reliability, and legacy integration are unsolved. Multiples in this bucket price in adoption that hasn't happened.
Quantum: real progress, no commercial winner. No modality has solved scaling; IBM's fault-tolerance target is 2029; today's systems generally can't beat classical on real business problems. Near-term value is in sensing, communications, and post-quantum cryptography — not compute.
AI drug discovery: candidates arrive faster, but wet-lab validation, trials, manufacturing, and reimbursement don't compress. Be skeptical of "AI biotech" multiples (relevant to ABCL, TEM). Regeneron, AstraZeneca/Daiichi and J&J advanced-modality approvals show where real value is landing — in engineered therapeutics, not platforms.
Immersive reality: headsets stalled; the category is now AI smart glasses (Meta Ray-Ban Display, Google Gemini glasses, EssilorLuxottica). Privacy, form factor, and "incremental value over a smartphone" remain open questions.
Connectivity and mobility: capital is flat-to-down. Monetization beyond basic connectivity (slicing, network APIs) is early; EV growth is real but regional and margin-pressured by Chinese cost advantages (batteries up to 40% cheaper).
