Q2 FY27 (quarter ended Jul 31, 2026) delivered revenue of $427.3M (+38% YoY), Subscription ARR of $1.66B (+33%), non-GAAP operating income of $33.5M (vs. a $4.4M loss a year ago), and free cash flow of $65.7M. The stock is a bet that the second act is already working.

▾

Industry & TAM

Rubrik sells cyber resilience: the ability to recover data and identities after an attack. It competes where data protection (Cohesity+Veritas, Veeam, Commvault) meets security (CrowdStrike, Zscaler, Okta, the hyperscalers). Rubrik's own investor deck gives no TAM figure or market CAGR, and we did not find an independently verified one in this run — so we triangulate from peer growth instead: Commvault's consensus 3-year revenue growth is ~11%, SentinelOne ~18%, Zscaler ~17%, CrowdStrike ~23% (StockAnalysis, Oct 8, 2026). Rubrik is growing ARR at 33% — roughly 2–3× the data-protection incumbent and well ahead of the security-platform peer set, i.e. it is taking share, not riding a tide.

Qualitative Analysis — Moat, Management & Culture

▾

Competitive moat — think systemically

Backups are the one asset every attacker tries to destroy first, because they are the victim's alternative to paying. That makes the recovery layer a strategic control point rather than an IT utility. Rubrik's moat is built from four reinforcing pieces: (1) immutable, zero-trust architecture — backups an attacker with stolen admin credentials still cannot alter; (2) a metadata index across on-prem, cloud and SaaS that lets it answer "what is clean, and what was touched?" in minutes, not days; (3) data gravity and switching costs — petabytes of protected history, compliance retention policies, and runbooks that customers rehearse; and (4) an expanding surface: identity recovery (Active Directory/Entra), now strengthened by Strata Identity, and agent-era controls via Rubrik Agent Cloud.

The flywheel: more protected data → richer metadata → better anomaly/ransomware detection and faster recovery → higher win rates and bigger net-retention expansion → more data. Evidence it works: net retention above 119%, $100K+ customers up 23%, subscription ARR contribution margin up 460 bps YoY to 14.0%.

Moat caveats. The category is crowded (Cohesity-Veritas, Veeam, Commvault), hyperscalers bundle native backup, and security platforms are moving into identity recovery. Management itself described competitors on the call only generically; Rubrik's investor deck lists CrowdStrike, Zscaler, Palo Alto and Okta as integration partners, not rivals — a relationship that could turn competitive. Moat width: narrow-to-moderate, widening.

Management & governance

  • Founder-led: yes. Bipul Sinha is co-founder and CEO; Arvind Nithrakashyap is co-founder and CTO (actively selling stock, see §2); Kiran Choudary is CFO.
  • Track record: since the 2024 IPO the company has moved from deep losses to non-GAAP profitability and ~19% FCF margins while growing ARR 30%+, and has beaten its own revenue guide by 5–10% in each of the last five quarters (§5).
  • Capital allocation: funded by a $1.13B convertible note (carrying value) with $1.75B cash and short-term investments; tuck-in M&A (Predibase — reported at over $100M — for AI model tooling; Strata Identity for identity). No buyback was mentioned on the call. SBC at ~22% of revenue is the principal "cost" that does not appear in non-GAAP results.
  • Culture & ethics: we did not retrieve employee-review or governance-incident data in this run, so we make no claims on culture beyond what the numbers show: R&D at ~30% of revenue, a disciplined guide-low-beat-high cadence, and a consistent conservatism on forecasts.

Operating leverage, margin drivers & cost scaling

This is where the business is strongest. Revenue rose 38% while operating expenses (ex-cost of revenue) rose ~14% ($277M → $315M), cutting opex from 89.5% to 73.7% of revenue — ~16 points of operating leverage in one year. GAAP operating loss narrowed from $94.5M to $71.9M despite gross margin slipping 110 bps (to 78.4%, on cloud hosting mix and AI investments). The drivers: renewal-heavy revenue (low cost to retain), cloud migration revenue with little incremental sales effort, and ARR contribution margin expanding to 14.0% (guide 15.5% for FY27). The risk is gross margin: cloud mix pushes costs up; sovereign/self-hosted deals push margins up.

Risks & Anti-Thesis — Steelmanning the Bear

▾

If we were short, this is the memo we would write.

1 · Valuation leaves no room for a growth stumble

Probability: 40%Impact: −35% to −50%Priced in: No

16.3× TTM sales and ~87× TTM FCF for a business with GAAP losses. Commvault, the closest data-protection comp, trades at 5.1×; Sentinel One at 8.1×. If Rubrik's growth converges toward the 15–20% security-software median, even a generous 9–10× multiple implies a stock in the $60s–$80s. Software multiples have de-rated sharply on one soft quarter before.

2 · The migration tailwind is ending — reported growth is decelerating

Probability: 50%Impact: −20% to −30%Priced in: Partially

Revenue growth went 51% → 48% → 46% → 39% → 38%; the Q3 guide implies ~23%. Adjusted net new Cloud ARR grew only 20% as legacy migrations wrap up, and Q2 net new ARR (~$96M) was below Q4 FY26 ($115M). Management's own implied H2 net new ARR (~$222M) is just +6% YoY.

3 · "Profitable" only before paying employees

Probability: 70%Impact: −10% to −20%Priced in: Partially

TTM SBC of ~$342M is 22% of revenue and exceeds the $286M of FCF; SBC-adjusted FCF is about −$55M. Weighted shares are up ~5% YoY, and Q2 SBC jumped to $101M (from $73M in Q1). GAAP net loss was $61.8M in Q2 and ~$254M TTM. Owners are being diluted to fund the FCF margin.

4 · Competitive compression and platform bundling

Probability: 35%Impact: −30% to −40%Priced in: No

Cohesity (merged with Veritas), Veeam and Commvault are all pushing "cyber resilience"; hyperscalers bundle native backup; and the partners Rubrik lists in its deck — CrowdStrike, Zscaler, Palo Alto, Okta — have every incentive to add identity and data recovery to their platforms. The identity-resilience push puts Rubrik closer to those partners' turf.

5 · Crowded positioning, insider selling and convert overhang

Probability: 45%Impact: −10% to −25%Priced in: Partially

The stock is +52% over twelve months and 68% institutionally owned. Insiders sold ~$13.8M in 90 days and ~$68M in twelve months while the lone insider purchase was at ~$47. The $1.13B convertible notes add potential dilution (they are already in the 226.8M non-GAAP diluted count) and settlement complexity. Short interest (6.6%) is modest, so there is little squeeze fuel either way.

Glossary & Methodology

▾

Each term is linked from the text above; use "↩ back" to return to where you were reading.

↩ back

Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) / Subscription ARR

ARR = annualized value of active subscription contracts

Rubrik's primary growth metric: what customers are contracted to pay over the next twelve months. Why it matters: it leads revenue; GAAP revenue lags because it is recognized over the contract term.

↩ back

Net New ARR

Ending ARR − Beginning ARR

New customers plus expansions minus churn in a period. Why it matters: the cleanest measure of current sales momentum — a falling number precedes slowing revenue.

↩ back

Net Retention Rate (NRR)

(Start ARR + expansion − churn) ÷ Start ARR

Above 100% means existing customers grow on their own. Rubrik reports >119%. Why it matters: high NRR means cheaper growth.

Free Cash Flow (FCF) & FCF margin

FCF = operating cash flow − capital expenditure; margin = FCF ÷ revenue

Cash the business generates after reinvestment. Rubrik reports its own FCF definition. Why it matters: cash, not accounting profit, funds the business — but see SBC below.

↩ back

Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)

SBC-adjusted FCF = FCF − SBC

Employees paid in shares. Excluded from non-GAAP earnings and FCF, but it dilutes owners. Rubrik: ~$342M TTM (22% of revenue) vs FCF $286M. Why it matters: negative SBC-adjusted FCF means owners fund growth through dilution.

Price-to-Sales (P/S)

Market cap ÷ trailing-12-month revenue

FTA scale: >30 Euphoric · >18 Excited · >8 Optimistic · 4–8 Aware · <4 Cautious · <2 Pessimistic. RBRK = 16.3× (Optimistic). Why it matters: the cleanest valuation yardstick for companies with no GAAP earnings.

Cyber Resilience

Prevent + detect + recover

The ability to restore data, systems and identities after an attack. Rubrik argues prevention and detection alone are not enough in an era of AI-speed attacks.

↩ back

Material Rights

Revenue recognized from options granted in earlier contracts

A subscription-accounting item boosting FY26 reported revenue that is rolling off (~$18M in FY27). Management therefore cites "normalized" growth (43% in Q2).

↩ back

Operating Leverage

% change in operating income ÷ % change in revenue; or opex as % of revenue

Rubrik: revenue +38%, opex +14% → opex ratio 89.5% → 73.7%. Why it matters: shows how incremental revenue converts into profit.

↩ back

Subscription ARR Contribution Margin

(ARR-related revenue − related COGS and sales/marketing) ÷ ARR

Rubrik's non-GAAP measure of the profit its installed base produces after the cost of acquiring and serving it. Q2 FY27: 14.0% (vs 9.4%); FY27 guide ~15.5%.

Flywheel

A self-reinforcing loop: more data protected improves detection and recovery, which wins customers, which brings more data. Evidence is NRR, customer growth, and contribution margin.
