Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

▾
Thesis (Contrarian Bull): Planet is the Western pure-play in Earth observation at the exact inflection where the data goes from "nice satellite pictures" to embedded, daily, AI-driven infrastructure for defense and government decision-making. Revenue growth has accelerated for four straight quarters — from ~10% to +58% YoY in Q2 FY27 — while adjusted EBITDA flipped from marginal to $13.9M (12% margin) and the company signed >$0.5B of multi-year satellite-services contracts (SKY Perfect JSAT $230M, Germany €240M, plus NATO, NGA and Swedish deals). Yet the stock sits 67% below its May peak, dragged down with the whole space complex after the June SpaceX IPO. At ~16x trailing / ~14x forward sales for a re-accelerating, EBITDA-positive, net-cash franchise, the market is pricing PL like a broken 2021 SPAC rather than the defense-backed data leader it is becoming. 1-year probability-weighted target $24 (+41%); 3-year $35 (+106%).

Planet reported Q2 FY27 (quarter ended July 31, 2026) on September 3. Revenue was a record $116.1M (+58% YoY), beating both the $102–107M guide and the ~$104.5M Street estimate. Adjusted EBITDA was $13.9M versus $6.4M a year ago; non-GAAP EPS was a first-ever positive $0.02. Management raised full-year FY27 revenue guidance to $430–441M and lifted the non-GAAP gross-margin outlook to 55–57%. The company ended the quarter with $865M of cash and short-term investments and essentially no debt.

This lands squarely inside the structural narrative in McKinsey's Technology Trends Outlook 2026, which argues that space has "gone mainstream" (the SpaceX IPO, Amazon's Leo constellation), that defense demand is the backbone of the space economy, and that Earth observation is moving from analysis into operational decision-making — citing Planet's Tanager constellation and its next-generation methane super-emitter detection by name. Planet is not a thematic side-bet on that trend; it is one of the two or three names the trend is actually about.

What the market is missing

  • The growth curve inverted. Planet spent FY24–early FY26 as a ~10% grower that the market left for dead. Defense and intelligence demand — a European rearmament cycle, sovereign-data mandates, and the JSAT deal in Asia — has pushed YoY revenue growth to 20% → 33% → 41% → 42% → 58% over five quarters. Consensus still models a deceleration the backlog does not support.
  • Profitability is real, not promised. FY26 was the first full year of positive adjusted EBITDA ($15.5M vs a $(10.6)M loss in FY25); Q2 FY27 alone did $13.9M. The company cleared the Rule of 40 for a fourth consecutive quarter (58% growth + 12% EBITDA margin = 70).
  • Backlog is a defense annuity. 98% of annual contract value is recurring; backlog is $815M and remaining performance obligations $753M, of which the German and JSAT contracts recognize over multiple years starting in FY26. This is contracted future revenue, not a pipeline.
  • Balance sheet is a fortress. $865M cash and short-term investments (up 219% YoY, partly from a $120M ATM raise executed near $32/share), no meaningful debt. Planet can fund Pelican and Tanager build-out and still weather a multi-year space-sentiment winter.

Microeconomic & Valuation Analysis

▾

Industry & TAM

Planet operates in commercial Earth observation (EO) and geospatial analytics. Third-party estimates put the commercial EO-data-and-services market at roughly $8–12B in 2026, growing low-to-mid teens, but the relevant driver is not the average — it is the defense and intelligence slice, which is re-rating sharply. European rearmament, the war in Ukraine, sovereign-data mandates (nations wanting their own guaranteed satellite capacity), and maritime-domain-awareness needs are pulling government budgets into commercial EO faster than the base market grows. Management frames its own TAM across defense & intelligence, civil government, and commercial verticals (agriculture, energy, mapping, insurance) as a multi-tens-of-billions opportunity; we use the narrower commercial-EO figure for penetration math and the broad one for optionality.

The structural point, echoed in McKinsey's 2026 outlook, is that EO is crossing from after-the-fact analysis into operational, daily decision-making: an intelligence analyst, a commodity trader, or a defense ministry increasingly wants a change-detected answer within hours, not an image to interpret next week. Planet's edge is its unique daily, global scan (the ~150-satellite SuperDove "line-scanner for the planet"), now paired with high-resolution Pelican tasking and a Planet Insights AI layer built partly on Anthropic's Claude.

GAAP P/E and EV/EBITDA are not usable yet: the trailing GAAP net loss (–$360M TTM) is dominated by a non-cash warrant-liability revaluation ($161M in FY26 alone) that increases as the stock rises, and adjusted EBITDA is only just scaling. The honest lenses today are EV/Sales relative to growth and EV/Backlog. On P/S, PL's 16.3x trailing multiple is a clear premium to the legacy data peers (Redwire ~4.6x, Spire ~6.6x) but a deep discount to the launch-plus-systems leader Rocket Lab (~53x). Given PL grows revenue 44% TTM with positive EBITDA and a defense-annuity backlog, we think the correct comparison is "premium data franchise," not "cheap SPAC," and the multiple has already compressed from >40x at the May peak.

Institutional ownership & insider activity

Planet has a dual-class structure; co-founders retain Class B super-voting shares, so control is concentrated with the founding team (CEO Will Marshall, President/co-founder Robbie Schingler). Institutions own a majority of the Class A float, with index and thematic-space funds prominent. The insider signal is a genuine negative: there have been persistent open-market sales and effectively no purchases. CEO Marshall sold 200,000 shares on April 6, 2026 at ~$35.07 and another 200,000 on July 10, 2026 at ~$25.92 (both under a 10b5-1 plan adopted July 2025), leaving ~2.70M shares. Directors have also trimmed. This is typical post-de-SPAC founder monetization and the plans predate the drawdown, but it is not a bullish tell, and we weight it in the conviction score.

The other dilution channel is the at-the-market (ATM) equity program: Planet raised ~$120M in Q2 FY27 at an average net price of $31.95 — opportunistic issuance near the highs that strengthened the balance sheet but grew the share count (296M → 318M → ~364M over the last ~18 months). Shareholders are being diluted; the question is whether growth and eventual FCF outrun it.

Analyst growth estimates

Consensus (11 analysts) rates PL "Buy" with a $33.40 average target (+97%). Views have split post-run: Deutsche Bank cut its target to $28 from $36 (kept Buy) in early September as the sector de-rated, while Clear Street trimmed to $51 from $53, and Wedbush has publicly argued for "buying the dip." The dispersion ($28 to $51) is itself informative — analysts agree on the trajectory and disagree only on the multiple, which is exactly where a sentiment washout creates asymmetry. Street models cluster around ~20%+ revenue CAGR over the next several years, which we view as conservative against a backlog recognizing over multiple years and a defense pipeline still expanding.

Qualitative Analysis — Moat, Management & Culture

▾

Competitive moat — a systems view

Planet's moat is not any single satellite — it is a proprietary, decade-deep, daily global image archive plus the operational muscle to keep it current cheaply. Three reinforcing layers:

  • The data monopoly of time. Planet has imaged the entire land surface of Earth almost every day for years, building a temporal archive no competitor can retroactively create. Change-detection and AI models are only as good as their history; Planet owns the longest, densest one in commercial EO. A new entrant with better sensors still cannot buy yesterday's picture of everywhere.
  • Vertical manufacturing & cost curve. Planet builds its own satellites in-house (the SuperDove line, now the SOMA factory expansion in San Francisco) at a fraction of legacy-aerospace cost, and replenishes the fleet continuously. That lets it sell daily monitoring at price points incumbents (Maxar-class) cannot match, while adding a high-resolution Pelican tasking tier and Tanager hyperspectral layer on top.
  • Data-plus-software lock-in. The Planet Insights Platform — with AI/agent workflows built with Anthropic's Claude — embeds Planet's data into customer decision loops (maritime domain awareness, defense change-detection, methane monitoring). Once a defense ministry or agency builds operations around a daily feed with 98% recurring ACV, switching means rebuilding a workflow, not swapping a vendor.

Competitive set: BlackSky (high-revisit tasking, smaller), Maxar/Vantor (very-high-resolution, private), Airbus & European sovereigns, Satellogic (low-cost challenger), Spire (RF/weather data), and the emerging shadow of hyperscaler and government-owned constellations (Amazon Leo, national programs). Planet's differentiation is the daily-global scan plus the archive; nobody else combines cadence, coverage, and a monetizable multi-year history at Planet's cost base.

Founder-led? Yes.

Co-founder Will Marshall (CEO, ex-NASA scientist) has run Planet since 2010 with co-founders Robbie Schingler and Chris Boshuizen; Marshall and Schingler remain executives and control the company through Class B shares. Founder control here is double-edged: it has kept the long-horizon "line-scan the planet" mission intact through a brutal de-SPAC drawdown, but it also concentrates capital-allocation and dilution decisions (the ATM, SBC) in a team that has been a persistent seller of stock.

Executive bench & capital allocation

Capital allocation has been disciplined-but-dilutive: Planet raised opportunistically via the ATM at high prices (a shareholder-friendly timing if not a friendly act), retired its legacy debt, and self-funds satellite build-out from a large cash pile rather than leverage. It has done tuck-in M&A (analytics/AI capabilities) rather than transformational deals. The strategic pivot of the last two years — deliberately leaning into defense & intelligence and sovereign customers, opening a London office and building out a German entity with a former head of the German Chancellery on the European advisory board — is the single best capital-allocation decision management has made, and it is the reason the growth curve inverted.

Culture & organizational traits

  • Mission & customer obsession: a public-benefit corporation (PBC) whose stated mission ("use space to help life on Earth") has, unusually, translated into commercial durability — the same daily archive serves defense, agriculture, and climate.
  • Innovation pace: continuous fleet replenishment plus three concurrent programs (SuperDove refresh, Pelican high-res, Tanager hyperspectral) — Tanager-2 and 18 SuperDoves shipped to the launch site in Aug 2026.
  • Talent density & ethics watch-items: a ~1,000-person, engineering-dense org. Governance flags: dual-class control, steady insider selling, ATM dilution, and stock-based comp — none disqualifying, all worth monitoring.
  • Structural traits: decentralized go-to-market by region/vertical, with the sovereign-defense motion now the growth engine and the commercial/agriculture base providing recurring ballast.

Quantitative Analysis — Financial Deep Dive

▾

Revenue streams, explained simply

Planet sells data subscriptions and satellite services: recurring access to its imagery/analytics via the Planet Insights Platform, plus increasingly large multi-year "satellite services" contracts where a government (Germany, JSAT/Japan) pays for dedicated capacity, direct downlink, and tasking on Planet's fleet. Roughly 98% of annual contract value is recurring, which is why backlog and RPO matter more here than a typical hardware company. Revenue is split across defense & intelligence (now the growth driver), civil government, and commercial verticals; geographically it is diversifying fast toward EMEA and APAC on the back of the sovereign contracts.

Risks & Anti-Thesis — Steelmanning the Bear

▾

If we were short PL, these are the five arguments we would lead with, in order of strength.

1. Customer concentration & lumpy sovereign demand

Probability 45%Impact HighPriced in Partially

The re-acceleration is powered by a handful of very large government contracts — Germany (€240M), SKY Perfect JSAT ($230M), NATO, NGA, Sweden. That is wonderful when it is signing and terrifying when it is renewing: a single large customer choosing to insource, delay, or not renew at size could swing a full year of growth. Sovereign budgets are political and cyclical; a change in the European rearmament posture or a U.S. administration's space-industrial policy (note the September 2026 White House pressure on firms to skip the Paris space summit) could reprice the whole thesis.

Our rebuttal: 98% recurring ACV and multi-year RPO mean the annuity is contracted, not hoped-for; and the customer list is diversifying (Rwanda national program, London/Germany build-out, commercial verticals). But concentration is the risk that would break the thesis fastest, and it deserves the top slot.

2. Space-sector sentiment & a 2.11 beta

Probability 60%Impact MediumPriced in Mostly

PL fell ~67% from its May $51.76 high largely because of the sector: the June SpaceX IPO sucked capital toward the mega-cap and re-rated the smaller space names downward, and PL trades with a 2.11 beta. Fundamentals barely feature in a sentiment unwind. If the space-stock winter continues — or if a high-profile launch failure or funding scare hits a peer — PL can fall to the bear case regardless of its own execution.

Our rebuttal: this cuts both ways: the same beta that punished PL on the way down amplifies the recovery, and the fundamentals (growth, EBITDA, backlog) improved through the drawdown. Sentiment risk is real but it is the least fundamental of the five.

3. Backlog and RPO are declining sequentially

Probability 40%Impact MediumPriced in No

Backlog went $900M (Jan) → $906M (Apr) → $815M (Jul), and RPO $852M → $816M → $753M. The company is now recognizing revenue out of the JSAT/German mega-deals faster than it is signing new ones of comparable size. If bookings do not replenish, the +58% growth rate is mechanically borrowing from a shrinking backlog and reverts as those contracts amortize.

Our rebuttal: backlog is still up ~11% YoY, the September 7-figure European deal and the NGA/Swedish wins post-date the Q2 cut, and lumpy backlog is normal when a few contracts dominate. But this is the single cleanest leading indicator to watch each quarter, and right now it is pointing the wrong way sequentially.

4. Dilution: ATM + SBC + warrants

Probability 70%Impact MediumPriced in Partially

Shares outstanding rose from ~296M (Jan 2025) to ~364M — via a $120M ATM raise (near the highs, to be fair), stock-based comp, and earnout-share vesting. Meanwhile the GAAP net loss is dominated by a non-cash warrant-liability revaluation that makes every screen look worse as the stock recovers. Insiders, including CEO Marshall, have been consistent sellers. A shareholder must grow enterprise value faster than the ~4–6%/yr share-count creep just to stand still.

Our rebuttal: the ATM was opportunistic (issuing high, not low) and left a fortress balance sheet that funds the roadmap without forced dilution into weakness. As revenue scales, SBC-as-%-of-revenue should fall. Still, dilution is a genuine, ongoing cost we cap the multiple for.

5. Margin structure & the "big-contract" trade-off

Probability 35%Impact MediumPriced in Yes

The very deals driving growth (dedicated-capacity satellite services) carry lower gross margins during their build/deploy phase than legacy pure-data subscriptions — non-GAAP gross margin slid from the mid-60s to the high-50s. If the mix keeps shifting toward capacity/services and those contracts don't mature into their higher-margin recurring phase as promised, the EBITDA and FCF ramp underwhelms even if revenue grows.

Our rebuttal: management guides non-GAAP GM back to 55–57% for FY27 and higher thereafter as contracts season, and the incremental EBITDA in Q2 ($6.4M → $13.9M YoY) suggests operating leverage is intact. The market already saw the margin dip and sold it.

Other risks noted
Dual-class founder control and steady insider selling; competition from hyperscaler (Amazon Leo) and government-owned constellations; execution risk on Pelican/Tanager fleet deployment and launch cadence; FX exposure on European contracts; the possibility that AI/Insights monetization stays a story rather than a line item; and macro/defense-budget cyclicality.

Glossary & Methodology

Methodology

All financial figures come from Planet's SEC-filed 8-K / Business Wire earnings releases (Q4 & full-year FY26, Q1 FY27, Q2 FY27, plus prior-year comparatives), the Q2 FY27 earnings materials, and market data from StockAnalysis / S&P Global as of the September 17–18, 2026 close. Several quarterly line items (Q3 FY25, Q1 FY26, Q3 FY26 gross margins and adjusted EBITDA) are derived from disclosed full-year totals and growth rates and are marked approximate. Peer data for RKLB, RDW, BKSY, SATL and SPIR came from secondary sources and is marked approximate. The thematic framing draws on McKinsey's Technology Trends Outlook 2026. Scenario targets apply EV/Sales multiples to FTA's own revenue and margin model; probabilities are judgmental. Nothing here is investment advice.

Earth Observation (EO)

Commercial imaging and sensing of the Earth from satellites, sold as data/analytics

Planet's core market: daily, global imagery plus analytics delivered via an online platform. Why it matters: the category is shifting from after-the-fact analysis to daily operational decision-making, which is the structural thesis.

↑ Back to Section 1

Backlog & Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO)

Backlog = total contracted value not yet recognized; RPO = the ASC 606 subset of contracted revenue

Planet reports both; ~46% of RPO is expected within 12 months. Why it matters: with 98% recurring ACV, backlog/RPO are the clearest read on contracted future revenue — and a sequential decline (Q2 FY27) is an early warning to watch.

↑ Back to Section 4

Adjusted EBITDA

Net loss before interest, tax, D&A, and further adjusted for SBC, warrant-fair-value changes, restructuring, litigation and earnout payroll taxes

Planet's headline profitability metric while GAAP earnings are distorted by non-cash items. Why it matters: FY26 was the first positive year ($15.5M); Q2 FY27 did $13.9M — evidence the growth is profitable.

↑ Back to Section 1

Rule of 40

YoY revenue growth % + adjusted EBITDA margin % ≥ 40

A balance-of-growth-and-profit heuristic. Why it matters: Planet cleared it for a fourth straight quarter (58% + 12% = 70 in Q2 FY27), rare for a company still priced like a broken SPAC.

↑ Back to Section 1

Price-to-Sales (P/S) and the FTA Scale

P/S = Market cap ÷ TTM revenue · Scale: >30 Euphoric · >18 Excited · >8 Optimistic · 4–8 Aware · <4 Cautious · <2 Pessimistic

Useful for growth companies where P/E fails. Why it matters: PL at 16.3x sits in the "Excited" band — a premium that must be earned by sustained 40%+ growth and margin expansion.

↑ Back to Section 2

Warrant-Liability Revaluation

Non-cash gain/loss from marking outstanding warrants to fair value each period

Planet's de-SPAC warrants are carried as a liability; when the stock rises, the liability rises and the company books a non-cash loss (–$161M in FY26). Why it matters: it makes GAAP net income and P/E meaningless here, and is the main reason to use adjusted EBITDA and FCF.

↑ Back to Section 2

Share Dilution & the ATM

Growth in shares outstanding from SBC, earnouts, and at-the-market (ATM) equity issuance

An ATM lets a company sell new shares into the open market over time; Planet raised ~$120M near $32/share. Why it matters: share count rose ~296M → ~364M; enterprise value must grow faster than the ~4–6%/yr creep for shareholders to gain.

↑ Back to Section 4

SuperDove / PlanetScope

Planet's fleet of ~150+ small satellites imaging Earth's landmass daily at ~3–4m resolution

The "line-scanner for the planet" that produces the daily-global archive. Why it matters: the cadence and coverage — plus a decade-deep history — are the core moat competitors cannot retroactively replicate.

Pelican

Planet's next-generation high-resolution tasking satellites, successor to SkySat

Higher image quality, more spectral bands, and lower latency than SkySat; underpins the JSAT constellation. Pelican-11 (tech demo) launched July 2026. Why it matters: adds a premium, taskable tier on top of the daily scan and anchors the largest contract.

Tanager

Planet's hyperspectral satellites (with Carbon Mapper / NASA JPL) for greenhouse-gas detection

Tanager-1 (launched 2024) has detected thousands of methane super-emitter plumes; Tanager-2 shipped to launch in Aug 2026, and new-generation units are planned. Why it matters: the exact capability McKinsey's 2026 outlook cites as EO moving into operational climate/emissions decision-making.

Planet Insights Platform (AI)

Planet's software layer turning raw imagery into analytics-ready insights, with AI workflows built partly on Anthropic's Claude

Announced March 2025; embeds Planet's data into customer decision loops (defense change-detection, maritime awareness, methane). Why it matters: the software/AI layer is the path to higher ACV per customer and is currently valued at ~zero in the stock.

CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)

CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1/years) − 1

Smoothed annual growth across multi-year periods. Why it matters: FY22–FY26 revenue CAGR of ~24% is accelerating (TTM +44%), vs low-to-mid-teens for the commercial-EO market.

↑ Back to Section 2

TAM (Total Addressable Market)

Annual revenue opportunity if a company captured 100% of its target market

Vendor-stated TAMs are typically far larger than third-party segment estimates (commercial EO ~$8–12B in 2026). Why it matters: use the narrow figure for penetration math and the broad defense-plus-commercial one for optionality.
